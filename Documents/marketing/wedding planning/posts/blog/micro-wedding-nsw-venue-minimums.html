Planning a micro wedding in NSW: what venue minimums really say

Topics in this guide

couplesvenuesguestsStage: Venue & dateNSWintimacyclarity

Plenty of couples want a small wedding: 20 guests, one long table, no fuss. Then they ring venues and find a minimum of 60. Small weddings are legally simple, but in Sydney they aren't always easy to book. We looked at what NSW venues actually publish about minimum guest numbers, so you know what to expect before you start.

Key figures

  • Of 25 NSW venues that publish a minimum number of seated guests, 11 accept 40 or fewer and 13 need 60 or more.
  • The median minimum is 60 guests. The range is 2 to 320.
  • Only 3 of 80 venues with a published seated capacity top out at 60 or fewer.
  • The legal minimum is just you two, your celebrant and two witnesses.

The myth: small is easy

Small weddings are cheaper in total, but not always per person, and the choice of venue is narrower than most couples expect. Many venues are built and priced for 80 to 150 guests. When a venue states a minimum number, it likely reflects the numbers it needs to make a booking worthwhile, though venues rarely say so.

The same dataset covers 176 NSW venues, but most don't publish a minimum, so these 25 are a small sample. We left out any value we had to estimate. Of the 80 venues with a published maximum seated capacity, only three top out at 60 or fewer. Most are set up for a larger crowd. The takeaway is simple: ask every venue for its minimum guest number and minimum spend before you fall in love with it.

The routes that work for small weddings

  1. Venues that publish a low minimum. Seven of the venues in our data accept 20 or fewer. Ask whether that applies to Saturdays.
  2. A public ceremony, then a restaurant. A council park ceremony and a private room are often easier than one venue for everything. See our park and beach permits guide. Note that Bayside Council limits ceremonies to 20 chairs, and several councils don't allow receptions on council land.
  3. Weekdays and off-peak. Some venues may be more flexible when they have a gap in the calendar. Ask, and compare with our budget breakdown to see if the saving is real.
  4. A private hire at a home, hall or restaurant. Check capacity, alcohol and noise rules.

Per head costs more when the guest list is short

A venue that charges a fixed hire fee or a minimum spend spreads it across fewer people. Our per-head data for 41 venues is in what a Sydney wedding venue costs per head. At the median starting price of $150, 20 guests is a $3,000 floor. But a minimum spend of, say, $8,000 would make it $400 each. We saw minimum spends published by only two venues, so the lesson is to ask rather than assume.

What a micro wedding still needs

The law doesn't care about the size. You still need an authorised celebrant, a Notice of Intended Marriage, two witnesses and the signed certificates. Everything else is up to you. See the legal minimum for the full list.

Your guest list is the real decision

The hardest part of a small wedding is who to leave out. Try this order:

  1. Start with the people you couldn't imagine the day without. That's your core.
  2. Add the people who would be genuinely hurt to be left out.
  3. Stop when you reach the number your preferred venue accepts.
  4. Think about a casual gathering later for everyone else.

Your turn: the micro-wedding check

  • Write your ideal guest number and your absolute maximum.
  • Ring or email three venues and ask for the minimum guest number, minimum spend and any weekday flexibility.
  • Compare. If a venue's minimum is above your maximum, cross it off now.

How we built this data

OmnyID Weddings keeps a directory of NSW wedding venues. For each one we record what the venue publishes on its own website and public listings. This snapshot is from October 2026 and covers 176 venues. Not every venue publishes every detail, so each figure states how many venues it is based on. Venue details change, so confirm with the venue before you pay a deposit.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need a venue to have a small wedding in NSW?

Not legally. You need an authorised celebrant, a Notice of Intended Marriage, two witnesses and the signed certificates. A venue is your choice, though public land may need a permit.

Do NSW wedding venues have minimum guest numbers?

Many do. Of 25 NSW venues in our dataset that publish a minimum number of seated guests, 11 accept 40 or fewer and 13 need 60 or more. The median is 60 (OmnyID NSW venue dataset, October 2026).

Are small weddings cheaper per person?

Not always. A fixed hire fee or minimum spend is spread across fewer people, so the per-head cost can rise even as the total falls. Ask each venue for its minimum spend.

What is the smallest legal wedding in Australia?

The two of you, your authorised celebrant and two witnesses aged over 18.
