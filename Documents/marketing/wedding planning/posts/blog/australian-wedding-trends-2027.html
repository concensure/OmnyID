Australian wedding trends for 2027: what to adopt and what to skip

Topics in this guide

couplesvendorsStage: Plan & budgetAustraliacuriosityinspiration

Trend articles tend to describe what looks good in a photo, not what suits your budget or your guests. This one is our forecast of where Australian weddings are heading for a 2027 date, extending directions that were already visible in 2025 and 2026. It is a judgement, not a survey, and we have not measured any of it, so treat each item as a prompt to ask your own vendors rather than a rule.

Bookings: 2027 dates are already moving

If you are engaged now, prime Saturdays in spring and autumn of 2027 at popular Sydney, Hunter and Southern Highlands venues are the first to go. Many couples are widening their options with a Friday, a Sunday or a late-afternoon start, which often comes with a lower venue fee or a longer package. Ask every venue what a non-Saturday date saves you, and whether public holiday long weekends carry a surcharge.

Guest lists are getting smaller, and spending per guest is going up

A trend we expect to continue is the 40 to 80 guest wedding with more spent on food, drinks and the room. The logic is simple: cost per head is the biggest lever in most budgets, and a smaller list lets you afford the things you will remember. Our guide to Sydney venue cost per head shows how quickly that number moves.

Food: shared tables and one memorable thing

  • Shared platters and feasting menus instead of a strict plated three-course. They suit long tables, and guests talk more.
  • Late-night food such as a pie, toastie or dumpling station, which is cheaper than a second dessert and well loved.
  • Local produce and one signature drink as a way to feel personal without a full bespoke menu.

Look and feel

  • Softer, earthier palettes: clay, olive, butter yellow and warm neutrals rather than a single bold colour.
  • Seasonal, fuller flowers and less single-use styling. Ask your florist what is in season for your month.
  • Long tables and candles as the main decoration, which also reduces what you need to hire.
  • Dresses with texture and personality: sleeves, bows, separates, short dresses for the reception and non-white options. See our dress guide.

Music and the evening

Expect more couples to pair a live act for the ceremony or cocktail hour with a DJ for the dance floor, and more attention to curfews and neighbours' noise limits. Check the finish time before you fall in love with a late-night plan: see NSW venue curfews and package hours.

Photo and video

Couples are asking for fewer posed portraits, more candid coverage and a short highlight film rather than a full-length one. Film-style photography and phone-friendly guest galleries are popular. Ask to see a full wedding gallery, not just a highlight reel.

A sensible way to start

Choose your first five decisions before you buy anything themed. A date, a guest range and a venue shape everything else. After that, pick one or two trends that match how you actually like to host, and let the rest go.
