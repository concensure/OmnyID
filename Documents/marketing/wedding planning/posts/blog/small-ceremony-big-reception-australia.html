Small ceremony, bigger celebration: how to plan an intimate wedding in Australia

Topics in this guide

couplesvenuesguestsStage: Venue & dateAustraliaintimacyjoy

Some couples want the ceremony to feel private and the party to feel big. Others want to save money or avoid being watched by 100 people during the vows. A small ceremony, with a larger reception on the same day or later, is a flexible way to do it.

The legal basics

In Australia, a marriage needs an authorised celebrant, a notice of intended marriage lodged ahead of time, and two witnesses who are at least 18. Everything else is up to you. Read our legal checklist and talk to a celebrant early, because they can tell you about timing and any paperwork. Our post on questions to ask a celebrant helps with the first conversation.

Choose a model

  • Private ceremony, party the same day. Ceremony with a handful of people, then everyone else joins at the reception. Good when you want the vows to be intimate.
  • Ceremony first, party weeks later. Gives you time to book vendors, spread the cost and invite those who live away.
  • Elopement, then an announcement and a casual party. Lowest pressure on the day, and often the lowest cost.

Planning the small ceremony

  • Location: a backyard, park, beach or restaurant room. Public land often needs a council permit, and fees and rules vary, so ask the local council early.
  • Order of events: a short welcome, vows, ring exchange, signing and a closing line. Fifteen to twenty minutes is usual. Our guest-friendly structure is to keep readings short and personal.
  • Music: a Bluetooth speaker with a prepared playlist is enough. Choose the songs, test the volume on site and ask someone to control it. Have a backup, such as a second phone or battery pack.
  • Sound outdoors: wind and waves can carry vows away. A small portable mic or a position away from wind can help.
  • Plan for weather: heat, rain and wind apply to small events too.
  • Photography: a photographer for an hour or two often covers it. Ask about short packages.

Planning the larger reception

  • Choose a venue with a flexible package, or hold the party at a restaurant, a hall or home with a caterer.
  • Decide whether to include a speeches and first dance moment, since guests did not see the ceremony.
  • Show the ceremony, even in a short slideshow or a read-out of vows, so people feel included.
  • Keep the budget separate so the two events do not blur.

Invitations and etiquette

  • Be honest and early: "We are marrying in a small ceremony with immediate family. We would love you to celebrate with us at our reception on [date]."
  • If the ceremony and reception are on different days, send the save-the-date to everyone and the ceremony details only to those invited.
  • Tell close family and friends personally if they are not on the ceremony list, so they do not find out from a photo.
  • Decide whether you will share photos from the ceremony before the party.
  • Set expectations about gifts. Many guests will give something either way.

A quick check

Ask yourself who you would be sad not to have at the ceremony, and who you would simply be glad to see at the party. Those two lists are your decision. Everyone else will be happy to be asked to a party that is relaxed and clear about what it is.
