How to write a wedding speech: how long, what order and a structure for each speaker

Topics in this guide

couplesMCswedding partyguestsStage: Final prepnervesconfidence

Being asked to give a wedding speech is an honour, and a small panic. Most people don't know how long to talk for, what to include, or what to leave out. The good news is that there are widely used norms for all three. This guide gives the lengths and order from an Australian source, a simple structure for each speaker, and a rehearsal routine that stops you running long.

Key figures (Bridebook Australia)

  • Most wedding speeches last 5 to 7 minutes. The best man's ideal is about 5.
  • The whole speech segment usually wraps up within 20 to 30 minutes and should take no more than 40.
  • Speeches traditionally go after the main course, before dessert.
  • Divide total time by the number of speakers to set each person's limit.

How long, and in what order

Suggested length and traditional order of wedding speeches (Bridebook Australia)
SpeakerSuggested lengthUsual position
Father of the bride7 to 9 minutes, no more than 12First
Groom6 to 8 minutes, no more than 10Second
Best manAbout 5 minutesThird
Maid of honour4 to 6 minutesFlexible
BridesmaidAbout 5 minutes, less if several speakAfter the maid of honour
Mother of the groom3 to 5 minutesFlexible, often near the end

Source: Bridebook Australia, "How Long Should a Wedding Speech Be?", checked 27 October 2026. These are conventions, not rules, and modern weddings often change the order.

Its practical tip: divide the total speech time by the number of speakers. Thirty minutes for five speakers is about 5.5 minutes each, with a 30-second pause between them. If you have more speakers than time, ask people to go shorter, not the couple to extend the night.

A structure that works

Bridebook's outlines for each speaker are similar. Here's a combined version you can adapt:

  1. Welcome and introduce yourself. Say who you are and how you know the couple. Many guests won't know.
  2. A short, warm opening. One line that gets a smile.
  3. One story, not five. Pick a single moment that shows the person's character. It's easier to follow and to remember.
  4. The couple together. Say something specific about what you've seen when they're with each other.
  5. Thank the right people. For parents and the groom, that usually includes the families and the wedding team.
  6. Close with a toast. Ask guests to raise their glasses and name the couple.

Bridebook's role-specific points: the father of the bride might thank the wedding team and pay tribute to his daughter; the groom might thank both sets of parents and recap his vows; the best man might add a playful roast of the groom and the groomsmen's well-wishes; the maid of honour can share anecdotes and relationship advice.

What to leave out (our view)

This is advice from our judgement, not a measured result:

  • Stories only half the room will understand.
  • Anything that would embarrass the person in front of their grandparents or boss.
  • Detailed history of previous partners.
  • Reading from a phone with a screen that locks mid-sentence. Print it.

Rehearse out loud, with a timer

  1. Write it, then read it out loud. A page of text doesn't map neatly onto speaking time.
  2. Time it. If it runs over, cut the weakest story, not the thank-yous.
  3. Say it once to one person you trust and ask: "What did you remember?"
  4. Practise the first and last 30 seconds until you don't need the page.
  5. On the day, keep water nearby and breathe before the first line.

If you're the couple: help your speakers

  • Tell speakers the order and their time limit in advance.
  • Give the MC a list of names and pronunciations.
  • Decide where speeches fit in the run sheet. Our guide to venue curfews and package hours shows why timing matters.
  • Be clear if any topics are off limits.

Your turn: the five-minute speech test

  • Write one sentence about the couple that only you could say.
  • Pick one story that proves it.
  • Read both aloud and time it. If it's under four minutes, you have room for the toast.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a best man speech be?

Bridebook Australia says the ideal length is around five minutes, and that best man speeches usually come third in the order.

How long should the wedding speeches last in total?

Bridebook says the speech segment usually wraps up within 20 to 30 minutes and should take no more than 40.

When do speeches usually happen at a wedding?

Traditionally after the main course but before dessert. Scheduling them before the meal is generally not recommended.

How do you stop a wedding speech running long?

Read it out loud with a timer, cut the weakest story and keep one story rather than several.
