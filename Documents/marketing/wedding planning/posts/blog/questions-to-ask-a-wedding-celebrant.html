Questions to ask a wedding celebrant before you book

Topics in this guide

You've found a few celebrants whose websites you like. Now comes the part that actually decides it: the conversation. A short call, or a coffee, is the best chance you'll get to learn how a celebrant works before you've committed. The trick is asking questions that get past "yes, of course" answers.

If you're still at the stage of building a shortlist, start with how to find a marriage celebrant in Australia. This guide picks up from there.

Start with the basics

  • "Are you a registered marriage celebrant, and can I see your registration?" Celebrants should be happy to confirm this, and you can check the Attorney-General's Department Register of Marriage Celebrants yourself. It's a normal question, not an insult.
  • "Are you free on our date and at our time?" Ask early. If the answer is no, you've saved yourselves an hour.
  • "Do you take more than one ceremony that day?" Some celebrants do back-to-back ceremonies, which can mean a tight arrival time and little flexibility if you run late.

What a fair quote should include

Celebrants set their own fees, and they vary with location, day of the week, experience and how much of the ceremony they write for you. A lower number isn't automatically better value, and a higher one isn't automatically better service. What matters is what's inside it. Ask for the quote in writing and check whether it covers:

  • Meetings or calls before the day, and how many
  • Writing or personalising your ceremony, rather than reading from a template
  • Help with the Notice of Intended Marriage and your identity documents
  • A rehearsal, if you want one
  • Travel, if your venue is outside their usual area
  • A sound system and microphone for outdoor ceremonies
  • Lodging your paperwork with the registry after the wedding
  • Your commemorative certificate (not the same as your official certificate)

Then ask about money: how much is the deposit, when is the balance due, and what happens to the deposit if you change the date or cancel. Clear answers here are a good sign of how organised they'll be later.

Questions that reveal fit

  • "How do you build a ceremony around a couple?" You're listening for process. Good celebrants ask about your story, your families and what you don't want, and they have a clear way of turning that into a script.
  • "Can we see a few sample ceremonies or hear a recording?" Tone is hard to judge from a website. A sample tells you whether their style is warm, funny, formal or quiet, and whether that's yours.
  • "How much say will we have in the wording?" Some couples want to write their own vows. Others want the celebrant to do almost everything. Make sure their approach matches.
  • "What happens if it rains?" Outdoor ceremonies need a plan B. A celebrant who has handled weather before will have an easy answer.
  • "What's your plan if you're unwell on the day?" It's uncomfortable to ask but worth it. Find out whether they have a trusted colleague to step in and how the paperwork would be handled.
  • "Have you worked at our venue before?" Familiarity with the space means fewer surprises with timing, acoustics and where to stand.

Red flags worth walking away from

  • They can't or won't confirm their registration. Stop here.
  • Vague about what's included. If you can't tell what you're paying for, you can't compare it.
  • Pressure to pay in full immediately, or to decide on the spot.
  • Slow or unclear replies before you've booked. Response time before the deposit is usually the best it will ever be.
  • A one-size-fits-all script. If every ceremony sounds the same, yours probably will too.
The best celebrant for you is the one you'd be happy to have a long conversation with, because that's what the ceremony is.

Trust your gut, then put it in writing

After you've spoken to two or three celebrants, you'll usually know. Notice who made you feel relaxed, who listened more than they talked, and who answered the uncomfortable questions plainly. When you've chosen, get the booking confirmed in writing: date, time, location, inclusions, fee and payment dates.

This is general information, not legal advice, and we don't rank or recommend individual celebrants. Check specifics, including your Notice of Intended Marriage timing, directly with your celebrant.
