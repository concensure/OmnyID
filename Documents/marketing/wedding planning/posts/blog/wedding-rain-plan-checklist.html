A wedding rain plan that works: eight steps to settle before the day

Topics in this guide

Nobody wants to plan for rain at their wedding, and most couples find they need to. The good news is that a rain plan is mostly a set of decisions made early, not a lot of extra spending. This guide gives eight steps in the order that works, drawing on what NSW venues and councils say about wet weather.

The short version

  • Ask your venue for its rain plan in writing before you pay a deposit.
  • In our NSW venue data, 14 of 18 venues with a recorded plan have a fully equivalent indoor space. We recorded a plan for only 18 of 176 venues, so ask every time.
  • For council parks and beaches, check the refund rules. One council refunds only if it closes the venue.
  • Settle who decides, and by when, before the week of the wedding.

Step 1: ask what the rain plan is

Don't accept "we'll sort something out". Ask: where does the ceremony go, how many people does it hold, and is it the same space you've priced? Our venue capacity and wet-weather data shows what 18 NSW venues record: 14 have an equivalent indoor alternative, 3 a partial one and 1 none.

Step 2: check it really is equivalent

  • Is the indoor space big enough for your full guest list, seated?
  • Does it still look the way you imagined? Photograph it now.
  • Can you swap spaces without moving your timeline?
  • Does your decor suit it? See our decor guide for what to avoid fixing to surfaces.

Step 3: agree the decision deadline and the decider

Put it in writing: who calls it, at what time, and based on what information. A common approach (our suggestion) is a firm decision time on the morning of the wedding, with a "heads-up" the day before. Use the Bureau of Meteorology's forecasts, and decide in advance what counts as "too wet".

Step 4: if it's a public park or beach, read the cancellation terms

Some councils offer little flexibility. Northern Beaches Council's page says a refund is only possible if the council closes the venue or you give written notice at least two business days before the wedding. A permit usually covers a place and a time, not a backup. See our permits guide, and plan a separate indoor option for the ceremony if the park permit can't move.

Step 5: tell your suppliers the plan

Your photographer, celebrant, musicians and florist should each know the fallback space, the decision time and who to call. Ask for any changes in access, power and sound. Ask your celebrant whether their equipment works indoors and outdoors.

Step 6: plan the practical kit

  • A few large umbrellas for the walk between spaces. Clear ones photograph well.
  • Towels and a spare pair of shoes for the couple.
  • Door mats or a runner at the entrance.
  • For hot days, the same thinking applies. Easy Weddings suggests icy water at the ceremony, and parasols and fans in very hot climates.

Step 7: tell your guests

A line on your wedding website or a text on the morning is enough: where to go, where to park, and what to wear. Guests will take it in their stride if you do.

Step 8: check your contracts and insurance

If you need to move the date because of weather, find out who bears the cost. Extreme weather causing a postponement is one of the events wedding insurance typically covers, according to Finder. See what wedding insurance covers, and your rights on deposits.

Your turn: the rain-plan one-pager

  • Write the fallback location, its capacity and the decision time.
  • Name who calls it, and who tells each supplier.
  • Share it with your wedding party and suppliers a week out.

Frequently asked questions

Do NSW wedding venues have a rain plan?

Of 18 venues with a recorded plan in our dataset, 14 have a fully equivalent indoor alternative, 3 a partial one and 1 none. We recorded a plan for only 18 of 176 venues, so ask before you book.

Who decides to move a wedding indoors?

Agree this in writing in advance: who decides, at what time and based on what information. Many couples set a morning-of decision with a heads-up the day before.

Can you get a refund on a council park permit if it rains?

It depends on the council. Northern Beaches Council says a refund is only possible if the council closes the venue or you give at least two business days' written notice.

Does wedding insurance cover bad weather?

Finder lists extreme weather forcing a postponement, such as cyclones, storms or flooding, among typical inclusions. Check each policy's PDS.
