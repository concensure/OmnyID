Wedding favours should be cute and useful: a two-question test

Topics in this guide

couplesguestsStage: Details & invitescreativityjoy

A wedding favour has one job: to be taken home. The ones that stay on the table at the end of the night, a pile of little boxes and a lot of good intentions, failed that job. The ones that end up in a handbag, on a fridge or in a glove box passed it. The difference usually comes down to two questions, and a favour needs to answer yes to both.

The cute-and-useful test

  • Is it cute? Would a guest smile when they see it, and would they be happy to be seen carrying it?
  • Is it useful? Will they use it in the next month, without having to find a place for it?
  • Cute only: a keepsake that gets thanked and forgotten.
  • Useful only: a thing that gets used but never connects to your day.
  • Both: the one guests keep.

What the evidence says (and doesn't)

We couldn't find Australian data on which favours guests keep, and we'd rather say that than make something up. The international signals we did find all point the same way:

  • A UK study from Sainsbury's, reported by wedding sites, found 37% of guests don't eat edible favours.
  • A 2024 Zola study of about 7,000 US couples reportedly found 62% viewed traditional favours as "somewhat outdated" (we saw this through a secondary summary, not the study itself).
  • Inside Weddings advises avoiding items personalised with the couple's names or date, because guests have little use for them afterwards, and anything too bulky to fit in a carry-on bag.

Sources: Inside Weddings, "Wedding Favors Guests Will Leave Behind"; Boho Weddings, "Are wedding favours a waste of time?". International, mostly anecdotal. Treat them as a nudge, not a rule.

Sorting ideas with the test

Use the table as a starting point. Your guests and your venue might change the answers: a winter wedding wants different things than a beach one.

A few rules that keep favours in guests' hands

  1. Hand it over, don't leave it on the table. Put it in guests' hands as they leave, or at their seat with a clear "this is for you".
  2. Make it travel-friendly. If guests are flying in, small and light wins.
  3. Skip your names and date unless the item is genuinely lovely. The more generic it can be, the more likely it is to be used.
  4. Count what you'll actually need. Couples and families can share one favour, so order by household, not by head.
  5. Or skip them and say so. A donation in your guests' names, or a thank-you note, is a perfectly good choice. Our guide to personalised favours that tell your story has more ideas.

What favours cost

Favours are small items, but the line adds up across a guest list: Easy Weddings' 2026 report puts average bomboniere spend at $678 among couples who bought it. Ten dollars a head across 80 guests is $800, which is a lot to spend on something some guests will leave behind. See how favours fit into the bigger budget picture.

Your turn: the favour check

  • Write down your favourite favour idea.
  • Ask: would you use it next week, and would you be happy to carry it home?
  • If you answer no to either, try the next idea on your list.
  • Ask two friends the same questions about it. Their honest answer is worth more than a mood board.

Frequently asked questions

What wedding favours do guests actually keep?

Items that are both attractive and useful, such as reusable cups, drink sleeves, sunscreen sticks, hand fans or small jars of local food. We couldn't find Australian data, so this is based on international signals and our own judgement.

Are edible wedding favours a good idea?

A UK study reported by wedding sites found 37% of guests don't eat edible favours. Edible favours can work if guests can take them home, but check dietary needs and summer heat.

Should wedding favours be personalised with names and dates?

Inside Weddings advises avoiding items personalised with the couple's names or date, because guests have little use for them afterwards.

How much do couples spend on bomboniere?

Easy Weddings' 2026 report puts average bomboniere spend at $678 among couples who bought it. At $10 a head, 80 guests would be $800.
