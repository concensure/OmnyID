Wedding party or no wedding party: a practical guide for everyone involved

Topics in this guide

coupleswedding partyfamiliesStage: Details & invitesstressrelief

A wedding party is optional. You can have a large one, a tiny one or none at all. What matters is that the people you ask know what they are agreeing to, and that you look after them.

Do you need one?

No. Some couples walk in alone, stand with a celebrant and a few friends, or have a single person each. A wedding party adds support, photos and shared memories, but also cost, scheduling and politics. Ask yourself:

  • Would we choose these people if no one expected it?
  • Can we afford to support them if we ask them to buy outfits or travel?
  • Would leaving someone out cause a rift we would regret?

Who to include

  • Choose people you trust and are close to now, not only ones you were close to years ago.
  • Aim for numbers that suit the space and the budget. Equal sides are not required.
  • Mixed gender wedding parties are normal. Roles and outfits do not have to match tradition.
  • If family expect to be included, you can give them a different role: a reading, a toast or an usher job.

What to tell them up front

  • The date, location and which events they are expected at: engagement party, hens or bucks, rehearsal, wedding day, next-day brunch.
  • What they will pay for and what you cover. State this clearly, so no one has to guess.
  • The dress code and colour palette, and when they need to buy it.
  • Their jobs on the day: standing up, holding items, helping guests, speeches.

The colour palette question

If you have given only a colour and nothing else, it will probably work, provided the colour is flattering and easy to find. To reduce the risk, send a few example photos, give a shade range instead of one exact match, or let people choose their own style within the palette. Tell your florist and photographer the colours so everything works together.

Feed them

Wedding parties often get ready early, take photos and stay until late, and the first meal can be hours away. Plan for:

  • Breakfast or lunch while getting ready, including snacks and water.
  • A meal before photos, where possible, not after.
  • Drinks during the day, and enough water in summer heat.
  • A place to sit, charge phones and keep bags.
  • A clear plan for getting home safely at the end.

A guide for people travelling in

If most of your wedding party are travelling, a one-page guide helps. Include:

  • The schedule by day and time, with addresses.
  • Accommodation, check-in times, parking and transport.
  • What to bring: outfit, shoes, ID, sunscreen, a rain layer.
  • Local tips such as the closest shops, pharmacy and a good place for coffee.
  • Who to call on the day, ideally a coordinator rather than you.

Say thank you

A small gift and a personal note are a nice way to finish. The best gift is that they enjoyed the day.
