It should not take detective work

A slow reply is frustrating. An unclear reply creates even more work.

You are tracking availability, inclusions, minimum spends, travel fees, payment dates and what “full package” actually means—then writing another follow-up because one important answer is missing.

You are not difficult for wanting a clear price and a direct answer. A useful enquiry simply makes those answers easier to give.

A better enquiry loop

Six ways to get from message sent to a decision

Send a brief, not your whole thought process

Vendors can answer faster when the essentials are easy to scan. Lead with date or timeframe, location, guest estimate, budget context, priorities and one clear next step.

Try this: ask “Are you available, which package fits, and what would the likely total be?”

Ask for the same decision facts

Quotes do not need identical designs, but they should answer comparable questions. Ask each vendor for inclusions, exclusions, optional extras, timing and payment terms.

Try this: request a total estimate based on the same guest count and service scope.

Contact a real shortlist

Sending twenty generic messages creates twenty threads and very little confidence. Start with a small group that appears to fit your location, scale, budget and priorities.

Try this: choose three strong candidates and one backup, with a reason for each.

Build the shortlist before you build the inbox.

Start with your date, location, guest estimate, budget range and top priority.

Track replies where the decision lives

A response is not useful if it disappears between email, DMs and notes. Keep it beside the vendor, quote, last contact and next follow-up.

Try this: record replied, waiting, unavailable and needs clarification—not just contacted.

Follow up politely, once the timing makes sense

A short, contextual reminder is easier to answer than restarting the conversation. Keep the original date and key question visible.

Try this: schedule one useful follow-up, then decide whether to move to your backup.

Compare the likely total, not the headline

A lower starting price can become the expensive option after staffing, travel, overtime or required upgrades. Keep confirmed costs separate from estimates and unknowns.

Try this: compare expected total, must-have fit, cancellation terms and every unconfirmed extra.

Stay in control

“I do not want a bot speaking for us.”

Will messages send automatically?

No. The intended flow gives you a draft to edit and approve. A follow-up should not become a promise, booking or payment.

Will it invent missing prices?

No. Confirmed facts, estimates and unknowns should remain visibly different. Missing information becomes a question, not a guess.

We already use email and spreadsheets.

Keep using them where they help. OmnyID reduces the work of reconnecting each message to the quote, comparison, budget and next decision.

Will vendors hate structured enquiries?

A concise brief helps a vendor judge fit and answer the important points. It should still sound like you, not a procurement form.

FAQ

Before you send the next enquiry

What details should we know first?

A date or timeframe, location, approximate guest count, budget range and top priority are enough to start. Unknowns can stay unknown.

Can OmnyID guarantee a vendor reply?

No. It can help you send a clearer enquiry and follow up consistently, but availability and response remain the vendor's decision.

Is our budget and guest information private?

Your context is private by default. You decide which relevant details are included in an enquiry.

Does this work for small weddings and elopements?

Yes. It can support elopements, backyard weddings, small dinners, cultural multi-day events and large celebrations without forcing the same vendor list.

Can we change a draft before sending?

Yes. The couple should review the wording, recipients and shared context before any outward action.
