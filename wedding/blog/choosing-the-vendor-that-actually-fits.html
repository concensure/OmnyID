Choosing the vendor that actually fits, not the one with the best Instagram

It's 11pm. You have nineteen tabs open — six photographers, five florists, a handful of "top 50 venues" lists, and a review site that contradicts the other review site. Every vendor looks lovely. Every one of them is "passionate about telling your story." And you're no closer to choosing than you were at 8pm.

Choosing a wedding vendor isn't hard because there are no good options. It's hard because there are too many options that look equally good, and almost nothing that tells you which one fits your wedding.

More options make the choice worse, not better

Comparing twenty vendors isn't twenty times the information — it's twenty times the doubt. Every extra option is another "but what if the next one is better?" Couples end up choosing on whoever replied first, whoever had the prettiest feed, or whoever a friend mentioned once, then second-guessing it for months.

What actually helps is a short list of genuinely suitable options and a clear reason for each one. Three is usually enough to compare honestly without the comparison taking over your week.

A good shortlist isn't the three most beautiful vendors. It's the three you could book without regret, with the reasons written down.

What "best fit" actually means

Polish is easy to see. Fit is not. A vendor fits when they match the things that are hard to change later:

  • Hard constraints — they serve your area, cover your guest count, and work within your realistic budget range.
  • Availability — they're free on your date, or at least haven't told you they aren't. Unknown is fine; assumed is not.
  • What you care about — the atmosphere you described, the priorities you set, the things you said you'd happily trade away.
  • Evidence, not adjectives — real pricing, real inclusions, and a real way to contact them, rather than a promise in a bio.

Most vendor directories rank on none of these. They rank on who paid for placement or who has the most reviews.

How the decision flow in OmnyID helps you choose

OmnyID Weddings starts from what you told it during onboarding — location, date or season, guest count, budget comfort, priorities, and the feel you're going for — and works through one vendor category at a time:

  • Three choices, not thirty. For the category you're working on, you get a shortlist of three suitable vendors. Ask for a different category and the shortlist switches to that one; earlier comparisons stay in your history.
  • Reasons you can read. Each card explains why that vendor is on the list, using your own answers — and says what's still unverified, such as availability on your date.
  • Honest about what it doesn't know. Unknown availability is labelled as unknown. If there's no verified photo of a vendor, you'll see a clearly labelled placeholder, never a stock image passed off as their work. If OmnyID can't find three suitable vendors, it tells you what's missing instead of padding the list.
  • One step from choice to enquiry. From a card you can check availability or request a quote. OmnyID prepares the enquiry with your details; you review it and send it yourself.
  • Quotes turn into a clear decision. When a quote comes back, it appears as an open decision with the choices that matter: accept, negotiate, look at an alternative, or defer. Each option sits next to the quote and the reasons behind it, so you're deciding with the evidence in front of you rather than from memory.

Deciding together without re-arguing it

Part of choice fatigue is relitigating. One partner remembers why you ruled out the second florist; the other doesn't. Because each decision keeps its reasons and the quote it was based on, "why did we pick them again?" has an answer you can both see. If something material changes — a new quote, a date that moves — you get a fresh comparison, and the earlier one is still there for reference.

Questions worth asking any shortlisted vendor

  • What exactly is included in this price, and what typically gets added (travel, overtime, GST, delivery, setup)?
  • How long is this quote valid, and what deposit secures the date?
  • Who will actually be there on the day — you, or someone from your team?
  • What happens if you're unwell or something goes wrong on the day?
  • Can you share a full gallery or a recent event, not just highlights?
