Getting married in a NSW park, beach or national park: permits, fees and lead times

Topics in this guide

couplescouncilscelebrantsStage: Venue & dateNSWAustraliauncertaintyconfidence

A ceremony on the sand or under the gums looks free, and it can be cheap. But in NSW it is almost never permit-free, and the rules depend on who owns the ground you're standing on. This guide walks you through the order to check things in, with the fees and lead times we could verify on official pages on 27 October 2026.

The short version

  • Find out who manages the land first: council, NSW National Parks, Crown Lands or a state parklands authority.
  • National parks: apply by email to the local park office at least 15 days before, or up to 12 months ahead for a better chance of your preferred spot.
  • Councils differ a lot. Northern Beaches charges $498 for up to three hours. Byron Shire charges a $130 application fee plus $281 for 1 to 50 people.
  • Almost every council we checked bans confetti, and many are alcohol-free.

Step 1: find out who manages the spot

This is the step that trips couples up, because one beach can have several managers. Byron Shire Council's wedding page is a good example. It says some sites are outside its control and need approval from the landholder directly:

  • Crown Lands: Torakina, Belongil, Broken Head and Seven Mile beaches.
  • National Parks: The Pass, Wategos, Little Wategos, Byron Bay Lighthouse, Tallows, Cosy Corner, Broken Head Caravan Park and Whites Beach.

Ask the venue's own website, the council's reserves page, or the park office who holds the permit. If the answer is "I'm not sure", assume you need one.

Source: Byron Shire Council, "Planning a Wedding", checked 27 October 2026.

Step 2: if it is a NSW national park

NSW National Parks says you can find a wedding venue across more than 40 locations in its parks. Its process, from its special events page:

  1. Contact the local park office to discuss booking a venue.
  2. For a wedding or small-scale venue hire with 40 to 150 people, submit the small-scale event application by email at least 15 days before the wedding.
  3. You can apply up to 12 months ahead, which gives a better chance of securing your ideal location.
  4. Events over 150 people fall under large-scale events and are applied for online.
  5. Check the NPWS fee schedule for small-scale venue hire and weddings. We could not read the 2026 fee PDF, so we are not quoting amounts. Ask the park office for the current figure.

Some parks list their own wedding terms. The Brisbane Water National Park page, for example, says a permit is needed to hold a wedding or do commercial photography. If you are hiring a photographer, ask whether they hold the right permit for that park.

A common thread: a permit gets you the space for a set time, not exclusive use, and not a reception. Plan the party for a licensed venue and the ceremony for the park.

Step 4: check the day-of rules

  • Confetti and rice. Northern Beaches bans all confetti, including biodegradable. Parklands and Olympic Park venues have similar rules. Use bubbles or a cheerful wave.
  • Alcohol. Many council reserves and beaches are alcohol-free zones. Don't plan a champagne toast without checking.
  • Decorations. They must be removed when you leave. Check what you can fix to trees and structures. See our DIY decor guide for venue-rule examples.
  • Cancellations. Northern Beaches refunds only if the council closes the venue or you give written notice at least two business days before. Ask what happens if it rains. Our guide to deposits and cancellation rights has the questions to ask.

    Frequently asked questions

    Do you need a permit to get married on a NSW beach?

    Usually yes. Councils such as Northern Beaches and Byron Shire require a booking or licence for a wedding ceremony on public land, and some beaches are managed by Crown Lands or NSW National Parks instead. Confirm who manages your spot first.

    How do you book a wedding in a NSW national park?

    NSW National Parks says to contact the local park office, then submit the small-scale event application by email at least 15 days before the wedding. You can apply up to 12 months ahead. Events over 150 people are applied for online as large-scale events.

    How much does a council beach wedding permit cost in NSW?

    It varies. Northern Beaches Council lists $498 for up to three hours. Byron Shire lists a $130 application fee plus a $281 licence for 1 to 50 people, $364 for 51 to 100 and $500+ for 101 to 150. Fees change, so check the current amount.

    Can you use confetti at a NSW park or beach wedding?

    Often not. Northern Beaches Council says confetti, including biodegradable, rice and balloons are not permitted in its open spaces. Check your council's rules.
