The vendor-chasing fatigue nobody warns you about

Ask a couple three months into planning what's wearing them down, and it's rarely the big choices. It's the in-between work: the enquiry you sent to four florists, the two that replied, the one that sent a PDF you haven't opened, and the one you're fairly sure you never heard back from — but you'd have to scroll through your inbox to be certain.

That's vendor-chasing fatigue. It doesn't show up on any checklist, it doesn't feel like "real" planning, and it quietly takes up more evenings than almost anything else.

Why chasing is so much more tiring than it looks

Sending one enquiry takes ten minutes. The tiring part is everything that follows, because each enquiry opens a small loop you now have to carry around in your head:

  • Who have we asked? Across categories, partners, and sometimes parents, it's easy to lose track of which vendors have already been contacted — and by whom.
  • Who hasn't replied? Silence is invisible. A missing reply never sends you a notification.
  • When is it fair to nudge? Too soon feels pushy; too late and the date is gone.
  • What did we tell them? Guest count, date, budget range — repeated slightly differently in every email, and then changed halfway through planning.
  • What did they actually say? Prices and inclusions end up scattered across email threads, Instagram DMs, and screenshots.

Multiply that by eight to fifteen vendor categories, and the couple becomes a part-time project coordinator for a project they've never run before.

The exhausting part isn't sending the email. It's being the only place where the status of every conversation lives.

The common fixes, and why they don't stick

Most couples try a spreadsheet. It works for about three weeks — until a reply arrives on a busy Tuesday and nobody updates the row. Others lean on inbox labels or a shared notes app. These all have the same weakness: they depend on you remembering to record what happened. The tracking itself becomes one more thing to chase.

The fix isn't a better tracker. It's a flow where the act of contacting a vendor is the record, so there's nothing separate to keep up to date.

How the vendor response flow in OmnyID works

OmnyID Weddings treats each vendor conversation as one continuous thread attached to your wedding plan, from first enquiry through to a decision. In practice, that means:

  • Enquiries start from the vendor, not a blank email. When you choose to check availability or request a quote, OmnyID drafts the message from details you've already given it — your date, location, guest count, and what matters to you — so you're not retyping the same paragraph for the fifth time.
  • Nothing is sent without you. You see the exact message before it leaves, can edit it, and send it yourself. There's no auto-sending and no surprise commitments.
  • Every sent enquiry gets a follow-up date. Instead of remembering who's gone quiet, the vendor shows up in your follow-ups when it's reasonable to nudge — with the nudge ready to review.
  • Replies land back in the same thread. When you connect your email, a vendor's reply is matched to the conversation it belongs to. Once a reply is recorded, follow-up pauses and the next step is to respond to what they said.
  • Quotes become decisions, not attachments. A quote doesn't sit in your inbox waiting to be remembered. It becomes an open decision in your plan, which you can accept, negotiate, set aside for an alternative, or deliberately defer.

What changes day to day

You stop asking "did we ever hear back from the photographer?" because your plan already knows. You stop rewriting the same enquiry because the details come from one place. And when your guest count changes, the next message to a vendor reflects the new number — not the one from your first email two months ago.

Most importantly, the status of every vendor conversation lives somewhere other than your head. That's what lets you close the laptop at 9pm and actually stop thinking about it.

A few habits that help, whatever tool you use

  • Put the essentials in your first message. Date (or date range), suburb or venue, guest count, and a rough budget. Vendors reply faster when they don't need to ask.
  • Ask for the same things from every vendor in a category. It makes quotes comparable later.
  • Pick a nudge rule and stick to it. Something like three business days, then one polite follow-up, then move on.
  • Keep one owner per vendor. If two of you are emailing the same caterer, decide who holds that thread.
