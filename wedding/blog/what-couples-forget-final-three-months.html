The final three months: what couples forget before the wedding

Topics in this guide

couplesvendorswedding partyStage: Final prepoverwhelmconfidence

By three months out, the big decisions are made, and what trips couples up is the small ones. Use this list as a prompt. Delete what does not apply, and add what does.

Three months to go

  • Marriage paperwork. Check with your celebrant that your notice of intended marriage is lodged in time and that identity documents are in order. See our legal checklist.
  • Vendor check-in. Confirm each vendor, their arrival time and contact number in writing.
  • Payments. List every payment due until the wedding and put each on a calendar.
  • Attire. Book fittings and alterations. See our dress guide.
  • Hair and makeup trial and the timing for the morning.
  • Accommodation and transport for you and guests who need them.
  • Insurance. If you want wedding insurance, buy it before you need it.

Two months to go

  • Send invitations if you have not yet, and set the RSVP deadline well before the caterer's deadline.
  • Draft the order of events and share it with the celebrant, the MC and the venue.
  • Plan speeches and who is speaking, in what order and for how long.
  • Seating plan first draft.
  • Music lists: processional, first dance and must-play or do-not-play lists for the DJ.
  • Rings and legal vows. Check the rings fit, and read the legal wording your celebrant needs you to say.

One month to go

  • Final guest numbers and dietary needs to caterer and venue.
  • Run sheet for the day, with names, times and phone numbers, shared with the vendors and the wedding party.
  • Rehearsal if you want one, with the ceremony party and whoever is leading it.
  • Assign day-of jobs to someone other than you: collecting gifts, moving flowers, tidying up, returning hire items, paying final invoices.
  • Final payments to vendors with receipts.
  • Name change and admin you want to do early, if relevant, and a plan for after the wedding.

The last week

  • Check the weather forecast and decide on any wet or hot-weather plan with the venue.
  • Confirm the time vendors arrive and who meets them.
  • Pack an emergency kit: pins, tape, blister pads, painkillers, stain remover, phone charger, tissues, snacks, sunscreen, deodorant, a sewing kit.
  • Bring the rings, vows and documents, and put them with someone reliable.
  • Prepare envelopes for any final payments or thank-you gifts, if you use them.
  • Charge your phone and set a handful of alarms.

After the wedding

  • Make sure the signed marriage certificate paperwork has been filed by your celebrant, then order the official certificate when you need it.
  • Return hire items and arrange who picks up leftover flowers and gifts.
  • Write thank-you messages and reviews for vendors who earned them.
  • Back up your photos and videos in two places.

If the list feels long, read how to reduce wedding planning overwhelm. You do not have to do it all yourself. Hand over what you can, and let the day be what it is.
