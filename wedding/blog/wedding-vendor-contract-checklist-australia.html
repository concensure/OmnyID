Read this before you sign: a plain-English wedding vendor contract checklist

Topics in this guide

couplesvendorsStage: Plan & budgetAustraliaanxietysecurity

A wedding contract is a promise in writing about what you will get, what you will pay and what happens if something changes. Most vendors are honest, but memories fade and staff change, so the paper is what counts. This is general information, not legal advice. For a large or unusual contract, speak to a lawyer or a free legal service in your state.

The clauses to find

  1. Who and what: the legal business name and ABN, the date, time, address and exactly what will be delivered.
  2. Price and GST: is each price stated including or excluding GST, and are surcharges (card fees, Sundays, public holidays) listed?
  3. Payment schedule: deposit, instalments and final payment dates, and which payment methods are accepted.
  4. Cancellation by you: what you owe at each stage. Many vendors keep the deposit and charge a rising percentage as the date nears.
  5. Postponement: can you move the date, for how long and at what fee? Is the original date kept in the meantime?
  6. Cancellation or failure by the vendor: a full refund of everything you paid, not only the deposit. Look for any limit on liability.
  7. Substitution: if the named photographer, celebrant, DJ or chef cannot attend, who replaces them and can you cancel without penalty?
  8. Overtime and extras: hourly rates, minimum charges and who authorises them on the night.
  9. Insurance: does the vendor hold public liability cover, and will they give you a certificate of currency?
  10. Force majeure: what happens in floods, bushfires, a venue closure, a public health order or another event outside either party's control? Insurance can cover some of these, so read the exclusions.
  11. Changes to numbers and details: deadlines for final guest counts, menus and timelines.
  12. Intellectual property and privacy: who owns photos, designs or recordings, and whether they will be used in marketing.

How to ask for changes

Most vendors expect some negotiation. You are more likely to get a yes if you stay specific and polite.

  • Ask in writing and for one change at a time: "Could the final payment be due 14 days before rather than 60?"
  • Offer something in return, such as an earlier deposit or a flexible date.
  • Ask for a written substitution and refund clause. Even a short sentence is better than none.
  • Compare several vendors' terms. If one is clearly stricter, you can say so.
  • If a vendor refuses to change a clause that worries you, treat that as useful information about how they will behave if something goes wrong.

Your rights under Australian law

Australian Consumer Law gives you consumer guarantees for services, including that they be delivered with due care and skill and within a reasonable time. Contracts cannot take these away. Rules on unfair contract terms may also help in standard-form contracts, though how they apply depends on the circumstances. For information, see the ACCC or your state fair trading office, such as NSW Fair Trading.

Practical tips

  • Do not sign under time pressure. "This price ends today" is a reason to slow down.
  • Pay deposits in a traceable way, usually by card or bank transfer to the business account named in the contract, and keep every receipt.
  • Save the final signed version, plus the emails agreeing to later changes.
  • Consider wedding insurance early, since cover for postponement or vendor failure often starts only after you buy it.
  • Put payment dates on one calendar so you do not miss any.
