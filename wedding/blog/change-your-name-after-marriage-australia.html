Changing your name after marriage in Australia: the order to do it in

Topics in this guide

couplesgovernment servicesStage: Wedding & afterAustraliauncertaintyconfidence

Most couples plan the wedding for a year and the paperwork for about a day. Then the certificate arrives and one question takes over: where do I even start? If you are taking a new surname, or combining names, the good news is that for a surname change after marriage the process is mostly free and mostly one document. The catch is the order, because a few steps depend on others and one of them takes weeks.

The one document that does almost everything

NSW Government guidance is clear on the starting point. You need an official marriage certificate from the registry (Births, Deaths and Marriages) in the state or territory where you married. It is also explicit about what does not count:

"Ceremonial or commemorative certificates are not accepted."

NSW Government, "Change your surname after marriage".

That means the decorative certificate you sign on the day (often presented in a folder) is a keepsake, not your proof of name. Order the official certificate from the registry as soon as your celebrant has lodged the paperwork. Registry processing times and fees differ by state, so check yours.

The order that saves you the most hassle

  1. Check your travel plans first. Flights must match the name on the passport you will travel with. If you are heading off on a honeymoon soon after the wedding, book in the name on your current passport and leave the passport change until you are back, unless you know the new one will arrive in time. This is the step that catches people out.
  2. Order your official marriage certificate. Nothing else can start without it.
  3. Driver licence. In NSW you take your marriage certificate and current licence to a Service NSW centre. Other states have their own roads authority, with similar requirements.
  4. Medicare. Update your card at a Services Australia centre, or by phone on 132 011 (the number NSW Government lists).
  5. Passport. The Australian Passport Office needs documents linking your new name to your birth or citizenship certificate. Its guidance says you may be able to get a fee-free replacement if you changed your family name through marriage and your passport still has more than two years of validity left, and that you should allow at least six weeks. Fast Track (an extra $107, five business days) and Priority (an extra $308, two business days) exist if you need the passport sooner. Your passport number changes when the new one is issued. Confirm current fees and rules on passports.gov.au before you lodge.
  6. Everything else. See the checklist below.

The "everyone else" checklist

NSW Government lists these as the places to update. Tick them off as you go:

  • Federal: Australian Electoral Commission, Australian Taxation Office, Centrelink and Medicare Safety Nets, where relevant.
  • State and property: Land Registry if you own a home, the Rental Bond Board if you rent, and the Pet Registry if you have a registered pet.
  • Money: banks and building societies, insurance providers and superannuation funds.
  • Everyday accounts: mobile, internet and utility providers.
  • Work and professional: your employer, payroll, and any professional registration you hold. These are not on the NSW list, but they are easy to forget.

When you do need a formal change of name

The free marriage-certificate route covers a surname change after an Australian marriage. NSW Government says you will need to apply for an official change of name if:

  • you married overseas, or
  • you want any other change, such as to a first or middle name.

Fees and timing sit with your state or territory registry, so start on its website.

Common questions couples ask first

Do you have to change your name when you marry? The marriage itself does not force a change, and the process above is for couples who choose one. Many couples keep their names, double-barrel, or combine. That choice is yours, and it can wait until after the wedding.

Your turn: the 10-minute name plan

  • Talk through what each of you wants. Keep, take, hyphenate or combine?
  • Write down your honeymoon travel dates. Will the passport change fit around them?
  • Put "order official marriage certificate" on the list for the week after the wedding.
  • Pick the three accounts that will annoy you most if they don't match (bank, super, employer) and do those first.

Which one are you most dreading? For most couples it's the passport, and that's the one to start with once the honeymoon is done.

Frequently asked questions

Is it free to change my surname after marriage in Australia?

NSW Government says it is not necessary to pay the fee for an official change of name when you change your surname after marriage. You use your official marriage certificate as proof. Other agencies may charge their own fees, for example for a passport replacement, so check each one.

What certificate do I need to change my name after marriage?

You need an official marriage certificate from the registry (Births, Deaths and Marriages) in the state or territory where you married. NSW Government states that ceremonial or commemorative certificates are not accepted.

Which should I update first, passport or driver licence?

Update your driver licence and Medicare first because they are quick. The passport needs at least six weeks, so plan it around your honeymoon. Flights must match the name on the passport you will travel with.

Do I need a formal change of name if I married overseas?

Yes. NSW Government says you will have to apply for an official change of name if you married overseas, or if you want a change other than your surname, such as a first or middle name.
