Lab-grown or natural? What Australian sources say about engagement ring costs

Topics in this guide

couplesjewellersStage: Plan & budgetAustraliacost anxietyjoy

An engagement ring is usually the first big purchase of the wedding, and the one with the least agreement about what's fair. The lab-grown versus natural debate adds a second layer: sellers on each side have a reason to tell you theirs is the better value. So we read what Australian sources say, noted who is saying it, and pulled out what holds up.

Key figures

  • $6,842 is the average engagement ring spend in Easy Weddings' 2026 report, tracked separately from the $38,252 average wedding.
  • Claims about how much cheaper lab-grown stones are range from 30 to 60% to 50 to 70%, depending on who is selling.
  • We found no independent resale data for either type. Every resale claim came from a seller or commentator.
  • Ask for a grading report from a recognised laboratory whichever stone you choose.

What couples spend

Easy Weddings' 2026 cost report puts the average engagement ring spend at $6,842, and says the ring is tracked separately from the average wedding spend of $38,252. That's an average among couples who bought a ring, not a recommendation. The right number is the one you can pay without borrowing against the wedding. Source: Easy Weddings, "How much does a wedding cost in 2026?".

How much cheaper is lab-grown?

The three Australian sources we read all say "a lot", but they don't agree on how much, and none gives a method:

The takeaway: a big saving is plausible, but the exact percentage depends on the stone, the setting and the retailer. Compare the same size, cut, colour and clarity from at least three sellers.

What about resale?

This is where the claims get heated, and where we found no data. A Melbourne jeweller quoted by Jeweller Magazine says lab-created jewellery has "zero" resale value. The Newcastle Herald third-party article says lab-grown stones "generally have lower resale value" than natural ones. Holdsworth Bros says natural diamonds "have historically maintained their value", though it gives no figures. All three are people who sell diamonds. If resale matters to you, ask any seller for their buy-back policy in writing, and don't rely on a general claim.

A decision test: four questions

  1. What do we care about most? Size and look for the money, origin, or a stone with family meaning. Be honest about it.
  2. Will we ever sell it? Most couples don't. If you won't, resale claims matter less.
  3. How will it be certified? The Newcastle Herald article advises requesting certification from recognised laboratories such as GIA or IGI, and says reputable jewellers clearly disclose whether a diamond is lab-grown or natural.
  4. What's the total cost? Include the setting, insurance, resizing and a care plan.

Questions to ask any jeweller

  • Is the stone natural or lab-grown, and is that written on the receipt and the report?
  • Which laboratory graded it, and can I see the report number?
  • What is your return, exchange and buy-back policy?
  • What does resizing cost, and how long does it take?
  • Is the setting's metal and warranty covered in writing?
  • Can I insure it, and what valuation do you provide?

Ethical sourcing matters to many couples. Whatever the source, ask for specifics rather than a general claim. A jeweller who can answer clearly is a good sign.

Your turn: the ring brief

  • Write a number you're comfortable with, then subtract 10% as a buffer.
  • Pick your top two priorities from size, origin, resale and design.
  • Get written quotes for the same specification from three sellers.

Frequently asked questions

How much do Australians spend on an engagement ring?

Easy Weddings' 2026 report puts the average engagement ring spend at $6,842, tracked separately from the $38,252 average wedding spend.

How much cheaper are lab-grown diamonds in Australia?

Published claims range from 30 to 60% (a third-party article) to 50 to 70% (a natural-only jeweller). None gives a method, so compare the same size, cut, colour and clarity from several sellers.

Do lab-grown diamonds hold their resale value?

We found no independent resale data for either type. The claims we saw came from sellers, ranging from zero resale value for lab-created stones to natural stones retaining value. Ask any seller for their buy-back policy in writing.

What should you ask for when buying a diamond?

A grading report from a recognised laboratory such as GIA or IGI, and written confirmation of whether the stone is lab-grown or natural.
