The venue tour: 25 questions that prevent expensive surprises

Topics in this guide

couplesvenuesStage: Venue & dateAustraliauncertaintyconfidence

A venue tour is short, the room is pretty and the coordinator is charming. That is exactly why people forget to ask the dull questions that affect the bill. Take this list, write the answers down on the day and ask for the same information in writing afterwards.

Dates, hours and access

  1. Is our date and time held, and for how long without a deposit?
  2. What are the start and finish times, and what does an extra hour cost?
  3. When can vendors arrive to set up, and by when must they be gone?
  4. Is there a noise curfew or a limit on amplified music? (More in our curfews guide.)
  5. Is there a surcharge for Sundays, public holidays or a long weekend?

Costs and payments

  1. What is the minimum spend or minimum guest count?
  2. Are prices shown including or excluding GST, service charges and credit-card fees?
  3. What is the deposit, the payment schedule and the cancellation or postponement policy?
  4. What is not included that couples usually end up paying for: chairs, linen, glassware, cake cutting, staff overtime?
  5. Can we see the last three quotes you gave for a similar date and guest count? (A venue will not share names, but a range is fair to ask.)

Food and drink

  1. Is catering in-house only, or can we bring our own caterer? If so, is there a fee? Our guide to NSW venue capacity, catering and wet weather explains the common set-ups.
  2. How do you handle dietary needs, children's meals and vendor meals?
  3. Can we bring our own alcohol, and is there a corkage fee? Who holds the liquor licence?
  4. How are drinks charged: package, tab on consumption or cash bar? See drinks options explained.

The space itself

  1. What is the real capacity for a seated dinner with a dance floor, not the standing capacity?
  2. Where do guests go in rain, heat or wind? Is the wet-weather option included, and who decides, and when?
  3. Is there air conditioning or heating, enough toilets, and step-free access for older or disabled guests?
  4. What is the parking and transport situation, and is there a safe place for taxis or shuttle buses?
  5. Is the venue also used by another event that day? Can we see how changeover works?

Risk and paperwork

  1. Do you require public liability insurance or certificates of currency from our vendors?
  2. Are there approved or preferred suppliers, and are we required to use any?
  3. What rules apply to decorations: candles, confetti, sparklers, hanging items, fixings on walls?
  4. What happens if the venue cannot deliver on the day, for example a flood, power outage or closure?
  5. Who will be our contact on the wedding day, and will it be the person we are speaking to now?
  6. Can we have the full terms and conditions before we decide?

On the day of the tour

  • Visit at the time of day your wedding would be, to see the light and hear the neighbours.
  • Photograph the toilets, the loading access and the weather option as well as the pretty spot.
  • Do not pay a deposit in the room. Ask for the quote by email and sleep on it.

Once you have two or three venues, line the answers up side by side using the same questions for each. If the numbers still look close, the one with clearer terms is usually the safer choice.
