Wedding songs in Australia: from the aisle to the last dance

Topics in this guide

couplescelebrantsDJsbandsStage: Details & invitesAustraliacreativityjoy

Most wedding playlists are built the wrong way round: a long list of songs you love, then a scramble to work out where they fit. It works better the other way. Decide the moments first, then ask what each moment needs the room to do.

We read through Australian celebrant, DJ, band and real-wedding write-ups to see how the day is actually divided and what gets played. The moments below are the ones those sources keep coming back to, not a fixed template.

What this is based on (and what it is not)

There is no public dataset of what Australian couples play. Streaming services do not publish wedding data by country, so every "most popular" list is a vendor's judgement. Here is what we drew on:

  • Celebrant picks: eleven Australian celebrants each named their aisle, signing and recessional songs for Ivory Tribe.
  • DJ and band lists: Sydney, Melbourne, Adelaide and Gold Coast vendors, including JJK Entertainment, Nova DJs and Wedding Singer Gold Coast, which says its list reflects requests at Australian weddings from 2024 to 2026 (no counts given).
  • Real couples: the songs chosen by couples in published Australian real weddings. This is a small sample of five, so treat it as colour, not statistics.
  • Trend pieces: Easy Weddings and a Sydney dance studio on what TikTok is pushing in 2026.

We could not find forum threads or social posts from Australian couples that were detailed enough to quote reliably, so we have not invented any. Where a song appears on several unrelated lists, we say so. Where it appears once, we say that too. Nearly every source sells something, so use these as starting ideas, not rankings.

The moments, in order

1. Guests arriving

Guests are finding seats and the mood is relaxed. Sources suggest soft, familiar and mostly instrumental or acoustic. One Australian playlist guide suggests Angus & Julia Stone's "Chateau" for this slot. Many couples skip it and let a live musician or a simple playlist run. It is the easiest moment to get wrong by choosing something you love that is too loud to talk over.

2. The walk down the aisle

This is the moment couples agonise over most. Two patterns show up across Australian sources:

  • Gentle and acoustic: "Can't Help Falling in Love" (usually the Kina Grannis version), "A Thousand Years" (Christina Perri), "Here Comes the Sun" (The Beatles), "XO" (John Mayer) and "Perfect" (Ed Sheeran). Kina Grannis's cover was the aisle song at two of the five real weddings we checked.
  • Current and personal: Alex Warren's "Ordinary" is the song most often named for 2026, and Easy Weddings notes couples practising their walk to it. Others include Best Part (Daniel Caesar and H.E.R.), Vance Joy's "I'm With You" and Leon Bridges' "Coming Home". Celebrants also list Aussie acts such as Lior, Ziggy Alberts and The Paper Kites.

Practical points: the song only needs to last as long as the walk, so ask for an edit or a start point partway through. Instrumental, acoustic or orchestral versions tend to feel more elevated. And if you are walking to a trending song, ask yourself whether it will still feel like yours in ten years.

3. The signing

Celebrants treat this as its own moment, and it is the one most couples forget. Guests chat, the music is background, and one celebrant notes that most guests will be talking anyway. Picks include Ray LaMontagne's "You Are the Best Thing", Leon Bridges' "Coming Home" and "Beyond", The Cranberries' "Dreams", Vance Joy's "We're Going Home" and The Paper Kites' "Bloom". If you kiss before signing, the music cues differently, so tell your celebrant which order you want. Signing songs are often short, so pair two.

4. The recessional (walking out as a married couple)

This is where celebrants say to go upbeat. Celebrant recessional picks include "September" (Earth, Wind & Fire), "Move On Up" (Curtis Mayfield), "Higher & Higher" (Jackie Wilson), "You Make My Dreams" (Hall & Oates), "You're My Best Friend" (Queen), "Everywhere" (Fleetwood Mac) and "Little Talks" (Of Monsters and Men). One celebrant says it "needs to be a banger", which is fair: guests are about to clap, cheer and pour out for photos.

5. Cocktail hour and dinner

Background music at conversation volume. Sydney DJ lists suggest a mellow mix such as Michael Bublé's "Feeling Good", Frank Sinatra's "Fly Me to the Moon" and Stevie Wonder's "Isn't She Lovely". A common tip is to start calm and build energy gradually, and to match the mix to your oldest and youngest guests.

6. The reception entrance

Opposite to the aisle: entrances want energy and a recognisable hook in the first ten seconds, because slow ballads stall a procession. Frequently listed picks include "Uptown Funk", "You Make My Dreams", "Shut Up and Dance", "Happy" and "Hey Ya". For an Australian twist, guides suggest "Down Under" (Men at Work), "Khe Sanh" (Cold Chisel) or "Great Southern Land" (Icehouse). If you plan a choreographed entrance, one Sydney studio says RAYE's "Where Is My Husband" is currently requested for exactly that.

7. The first dance

The most repeated songs across Australian lists are Ed Sheeran's "Perfect" and "Thinking Out Loud", "Can't Help Falling in Love", "At Last" (Etta James) and INXS's "Never Tear Us Apart". Newer entries are "Die With a Smile" (Lady Gaga and Bruno Mars), "Ordinary", Taylor Swift's "Lover" and JVKE's "Golden Hour".

Aussie options that come up: "Truly Madly Deeply" (Savage Garden), Amy Shark's "Adore", Dean Lewis's "Lose My Mind", Guy Sebastian's "Angels Brought Me Here" and Daryl Braithwaite's "The Horses". Among the real weddings we checked, couples chose INXS, Elton John's "Your Song", Frankie Valli's "Can't Take My Eyes Off You", and an acoustic Kylie Minogue cover.

Two practical rules recur. Studio tracks often run four to five minutes, while a first dance feels right at around two and a half to three and a half. Many couples invite guests onto the floor about a minute in, or ask the DJ to fade out at about 90 seconds. And listen to the full track, verses included, before you commit: a few popular songs have lyrics that do not suit a wedding.

8. Opening the dance floor

A DJ will usually time this for when the speeches finish. Songs that appear on both Sydney and Melbourne lists: "Uptown Funk", "September", "I Wanna Dance with Somebody", "Dancing Queen", "Shut Up and Dance", "Levitating" and "Blinding Lights". Current additions include RAYE's "Where Is My Husband" and "Die With a Smile".

9. The Aussie sing-alongs

This is the moment that is distinctively Australian. Vendors keep naming the same local songs as the ones that unite three generations at once: "The Horses" (Daryl Braithwaite), "You're the Voice" (John Farnham), "Never Tear Us Apart" (INXS), "Love Is in the Air" (John Paul Young), "Great Southern Land" (Icehouse), "Down Under" (Men at Work), "Khe Sanh" and "Flame Trees" (Cold Chisel), and "Need You Tonight" (INXS). Mix them with the global sing-alongs: "Sweet Caroline", "Mr Brightside", "Don't Stop Believin'" and "Dancing Queen". If you have a big family contingent from overseas, check which of these they will actually know.

10. The last song

Sources split this into two things: a high-energy crowd song, and a slower final dance. The crowd-song picks are "Don't Stop Believin'", "Sweet Caroline", "Mr Brightside", "Closing Time", "The Horses" and "You're the Voice". The slower farewell picks include "Never Tear Us Apart", "Last Dance" (Donna Summer), "Don't You (Forget About Me)" (Simple Minds) and "The Night We Met" (Lord Huron). Real-wedding picks vary a lot, so there is no single pattern. Whichever you choose: decide it in advance, tell your DJ, and announce it so people stay. One Australian registry blog suggests starting the closing song about ten minutes before the official end of the night, which also keeps you inside your venue's curfew (see our NSW curfew guide).

What the sources agree on

  • Different moments want different songs. Aisle songs are slow and short. Recessionals, entrances and the last song are loud and familiar.
  • The overlap is large. "Perfect", "Can't Help Falling in Love", "Never Tear Us Apart" and "Sweet Caroline" each appear on at least three lists. They are popular for a reason, but you will hear them at other weddings.
  • Some songs rarely fill a floor. A DJ warning we found: Beyoncé's "Crazy in Love" is hard to dance to, and slower songs late in the night can empty the room. Ballads work for moments, not the middle of the party.
  • Always give a do-not-play list. Every DJ source recommends it.
  • Brief your DJ early. Four weeks before is the shortest lead time any source suggested. Send audio files, not just song names, if you want a specific edit or version.

How to build your list without losing a weekend

  1. Write your own run sheet first: arrival, aisle, signing, recessional, drinks, entrance, first dance, dinner, dance floor, last song.
  2. Pick the three that matter most to you (usually aisle, first dance and last song) and let the DJ or band suggest the rest.
  3. Check each song's length, version and lyrics. Check it is available on the service your musician or DJ uses.
  4. Give your DJ, celebrant and musician the same document. See our DJ and band questions for what to ask before you book.
