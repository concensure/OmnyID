Why Australian couples spend about 23% more than their wedding budget

Topics in this guide

couplesvenuesvendorsStage: Plan & budgetAustraliacost anxietyclarity

Almost every Australian couple starts with a number in mind. Almost every one of them ends up above it. Easy Weddings' 2026 report, based on its survey of "4000+ engaged and recently married couples", puts figures on the gap, and when we ran the sums we found something the headline doesn't tell you. Here is what the data says, what it can and can't tell you, and a way to plan so the final bill stops surprising you.

Key figures (Easy Weddings 2026 report)

  • $38,252 is the average total wedding spend, excluding the engagement ring.
  • $29,471 is the average starting budget couples reported.
  • The report says couples spend "about 23% more than their original wedding budget".
  • $17,518 is the average venue spend, which the report puts at about 46% of the total.

First, a wrinkle in the numbers

If you take the two averages above and compare them directly, $38,252 is about 30% more than $29,471, not 23%. That isn't a typo. One possible explanation is that the two averages come from different groups of respondents, or that the 23% is the average of each couple's own overspend rather than a ratio of the two group averages. The report doesn't publish its method, so we can't be sure. The safe reading is: couples overshoot by somewhere between a quarter and a third, and that is the number to plan around.

What couples who bought each item paid

The same report lists average spend by category. One caution before the table: these are not slices of one $38,252 pie. If you add up every line they come to far more than the average total, because each figure is an average across couples who actually bought that item. Read the table as "if you buy this, expect about this".

Two things stand out. The venue is roughly half of the typical total, so it is where a wrong estimate hurts most (our own per-head data for 41 NSW venues shows how much the starting price varies). And the smaller items, each under $3,000, are the ones that quietly add up.

Plan for the gap, don't fight it

Telling yourself to "just stick to the budget" doesn't work when the typical couple overshoots. A better method is to decide the most you can comfortably spend, then budget below it on purpose. If couples typically spend about 23% more than planned, divide your ceiling by 1.23:

This is our arithmetic, not a forecast. Your own wedding might land lower. But if it doesn't, you will have already set the money aside.

Five places the overshoot tends to come from

The report doesn't break the overshoot down, so these are places to check based on how venue and vendor pricing typically works, not findings from the survey:

  1. Guest count creep. Every extra guest costs the full per-head price.
  2. Drinks upgrades and extra bar hours. "From" prices rarely include them.
  3. Extras billed separately. Styling, AV, hire, service charges and GST.
  4. Small things with no line. Alterations, postage, tips, transport, accommodation for the bridal party.
  5. Time. Late changes and rush fees cost more than early ones.

Our guide to budgeting without losing the joy covers how to track these without it becoming a second job.

Your turn: the 15-minute budget check

  • Write down the absolute most you can spend, and the date you would have to stop.
  • Divide it by 1.23 and treat that as your working budget.
  • List the three categories where you are guessing. Get a real quote for each this week.
  • Decide now which one thing you'd cut first if the total creeps up.

What was the number you started with? If you've already blown past it, you're in the majority, and the quickest fix is usually the guest list.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the average Australian wedding cost?

Easy Weddings' 2026 report puts the average total spend at $38,252, excluding the engagement ring, based on a survey of 4000+ engaged and recently married couples. Averages vary by state, venue and guest count, so use it as a benchmark.

How much do couples go over their wedding budget?

The same report says couples spend about 23% more than their original budget. Its own average budget ($29,471) and average spend ($38,252) differ by about 30%, so plan for an overshoot of roughly a quarter to a third.

How much of a wedding budget is the venue?

The report puts the average venue spend at $17,518, about 46% of the total spend.

How do I plan for a budget overshoot?

Decide the most you can spend, then divide by 1.23 to set your working budget. For a $30,000 ceiling that means planning for about $24,390 and holding the rest in reserve.
