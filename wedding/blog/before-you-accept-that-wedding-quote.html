Before you accept that wedding quote, make sure you are comparing the whole cost

By Casper Wu

Topics in this guide

couplesvenuesvendorsStage: Plan & budgetcost anxietyclarity

A wedding quote can look wonderfully precise and still leave the most important questions unanswered. The total is printed at the bottom. The package has a reassuring name. There may even be a neat list of inclusions. But is GST included? Does the number assume 70 guests or 100? Is furniture part of the venue hire? What happens to the price if rain moves the ceremony indoors?

That does not mean the vendor is hiding something. Wedding services are unusually dependent on date, guest count, location, duration and the choices you have not made yet. A quote is often a snapshot of a conversation, not the final shape of the day. The difficulty for couples is knowing which parts are firm, which are conditional and which have simply not been discussed.

The useful question is not only “What is the total?” It is “What would need to remain true for this total to stay true?”

Why two similar totals may not be comparable

Imagine two venue quotes that both land close to $20,000. One includes tables, chairs, setup, staff and a five-hour drinks package. The other has a lower food rate but leaves furniture, ceremony hire, security, pack-down and beverages as separate decisions. The totals look close today, but they describe different weddings.

A fair comparison first puts each quote on the same basis. That means identifying the guest count and duration behind the number, separating fixed charges from per-person charges, and distinguishing included, optional, excluded and unknown items. If the basis is missing, the honest answer is not a guessed total. It is a clear question for the vendor.

The items that most often change the final number

  • Tax and service charges. Check whether GST is included and whether an administration or service percentage applies to every line.
  • Guest-count assumptions. Per-person catering, beverages, furniture and staffing can all move together when the guest list changes.
  • Minimum spend. A room-hire fee and a food-and-beverage minimum are not always alternatives; sometimes both apply.
  • Time. Overtime, early access, late finishes, supplier bump-in and pack-down can carry separate hourly charges.
  • Place. Travel, delivery, accommodation, parking and difficult access may appear only after a site or schedule is confirmed.
  • Weather. A wet-weather room, extra structure, flooring or a second setup may alter both cost and capacity.
  • Commitment terms. A deposit, payment schedule, quote expiry and cancellation or postponement policy matter even when they do not change the headline total.

What Quote Intelligence can show you

The result separates directly supported facts from inferences and unknowns. It can surface:

  • the quoted total and the basis on which it was calculated;
  • items stated as included, excluded, optional or still unclear;
  • material terms that are absent or ambiguous;
  • conditions that could cause the cost to rise;
  • short evidence excerpts showing where each extracted fact came from; and
  • plain-language questions to send back to the vendor.

It does not decide that the cheapest quote is best, and it does not send an enquiry, accept a quote, book a vendor or move money. The output is decision support, not legal or financial advice, and it cannot guarantee a final price. A low-confidence result is a question list, not a verdict.

Start with one quote, then compare on the same basis

You do not need a perfect spreadsheet before this becomes useful. Upload the quote you are considering now. Look at what is confirmed and what remains open. If you receive a revised quote, compare the changed terms—not only the new total. When you later add another vendor, ask the same material questions so the comparison becomes fairer.
