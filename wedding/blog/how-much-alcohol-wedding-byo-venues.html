How much alcohol do you need for your wedding? A simple calculator and what BYO venues allow

Topics in this guide

couplesvenuescaterersStage: Celebrant & suppliersAustraliacost anxietyconfidence

Run out of drinks and everyone remembers. Over-order and you've paid for a recovery party you didn't plan. The good news is that the maths is simple. The harder part is that the venue decides whether you can bring your own at all, and in our data about half don't allow it.

The short version

  • Start with one drink per guest per hour. Easy Weddings calls this the general rule.
  • Plan one or two an hour for regular drinkers and two to three for heavy drinkers.
  • Of 16 NSW venues that state a BYO policy in our dataset, 8 allow it and 8 don't.
  • Always have plenty of water, especially in summer.

The rule of thumb

Easy Weddings' guide to wedding alcohol (published 2017, last modified March 2024) gives this baseline: "The general rule is one drink per hour per guest". It adjusts for your crowd:

  • Regular drinkers: one or two drinks per hour.
  • Heavy drinkers: two to three drinks per hour.
  • Non-drinkers: a couple of non-alcoholic drinks an hour, plus extras for thirsty guests.

Source: Easy Weddings, "How much alcohol do you need for your wedding?".

Do the maths for your guest list

These figures are our arithmetic from that rule, not a promise. They cover drinks served, not bottles. A guest list of 80 over a five-hour reception:

Not everyone drinks alcohol, so count your guests honestly. If a quarter of your guests don't drink, trim the alcohol total accordingly and add non-alcoholic drinks. Add a 5 to 10% buffer if you can store leftovers.

What to stock

Easy Weddings' suggested mix, for a standard bar:

  • One or two whites and one or two reds.
  • One sparkling.
  • Three beers or ciders, including one light beer.
  • Two non-alcoholic options.
  • One vodka and one whiskey, with gin, rum or tequila if the budget allows.
  • One or two signature cocktails.

Provide plenty of water throughout and offer icy water at the ceremony, especially on hot days.

Can you bring your own?

This is a venue decision. In our NSW venue dataset (176 venues, October 2026), only a minority record a BYO policy that is not an estimate:

Most venues don't publish this either way, so the n is small. Read it as "ask, don't assume". Our figure for outside caterers is similar: of 19 venues that state a policy, 10 allow external catering and 9 don't.

If BYO is allowed, ask:

  1. Is there a corkage fee, and is it per bottle or per head?
  2. Who serves, and are they trained in responsible service?
  3. Is a licence needed for the space? (Many public reserves are alcohol-free.)
  4. Can leftovers be collected, and by when?
  5. Do you still need to pay for glassware, ice and fridge space?

For park and beach ceremonies, see our permits guide, which flags the alcohol rules at councils we checked.

Compare packages, not prices

When a venue offers a drinks package, ask how many hours it covers, what brands are included and what happens when the package ends. Our curfews and package hours post shows how much time packages really give you.

Your turn: the five-minute drinks plan

  • Count guests who drink, guests who don't, and guests who really drink.
  • Write the reception's drinking hours.
  • Multiply using the table above, then ask your venue what its package covers.

Frequently asked questions

How much alcohol do you need per guest at a wedding?

Easy Weddings gives a general rule of one drink per guest per hour, one or two per hour for regular drinkers and two to three for heavy drinkers. For 80 guests over five hours that is 400 to 800 drinks.

Can you bring your own alcohol to a Sydney wedding venue?

It depends on the venue. In our NSW dataset, of 16 venues that state a policy, 8 allow BYO alcohol and 8 do not. Ask about corkage, service and licensing.

What drinks should you stock at a wedding?

Easy Weddings suggests one or two whites, one or two reds, one sparkling, three beers or ciders including a light option, two non-alcoholic options, a vodka and a whiskey, and a signature cocktail or two.

Do I need non-alcoholic drinks at a wedding?

Yes. Provide plenty of water, especially in summer, plus non-alcoholic options for guests who don't drink or who alternate.
