What wedding decor would you DIY? A practical guide to what is worth it

Topics in this guide

couplesfloristsvenuesStage: Details & invitescreativityjoy

Few wedding questions split couples (and their mothers) like this one: what would you DIY? The honest answer is "less than Pinterest suggests, and more than a hire company would like". This post gives you a recommendation, the reasoning, the Australian venue rules that catch DIYers out, and a way to test it before you commit your weekends.

The short answer

  • DIY the small, flat, make-ahead items: signs, menus, place cards, table numbers, photo displays.
  • Hire or buy anything big, tall, fragile or set up on the day: arches, backdrops, lighting and large installations.
  • Be cautious with fresh flowers and anything involving flame.
  • Run a one-table prototype and time it before you commit.

That is our judgement, informed by the sources below rather than measured from a survey. We couldn't find Australian data on which decor items couples regret DIYing, and we'd rather say so than invent a statistic.

What decor costs in Australia

Two benchmarks to hold in mind. Easy Weddings' 2026 report puts average decoration spend at $2,849 among couples who bought it (see our budget gap breakdown). Bridebook says couples in Australia typically spend between $1,000 and $2,500 on professionally sourced decor, and that this doesn't include flowers. They are different surveys and definitions, so treat them as a range, not a rule.

Sources: Easy Weddings, 2026 wedding cost report; Bridebook, "How Much Does Wedding Decor Cost in Australia?".

The four-question DIY filter

Before you say yes to a project, ask:

  1. Can it be made weeks ahead? If not, it will land on your wedding week.
  2. Is it low-stakes if it goes wrong? A wonky sign is charming. A collapsing arbour isn't.
  3. Do the materials clearly cost less than buying or hiring? Couples sharing their experience online often report that materials for some projects cost as much as the finished item.
  4. Does someone other than you set it up and pack it down? The two of you shouldn't be carrying centrepieces during the cocktail hour.

Three or four "yes" answers: DIY. One or none: hire it, buy it or drop it.

The venue rules that catch DIYers out

Every venue sets its own rules, so ask for the terms before you buy supplies. The published terms of some Sydney venues show what to look for:

  • Open flame. Bangarra Dance Theatre's 2026 wedding terms ban open flame on the wharf, candles included. Sydney Olympic Park's wedding FAQ says candles, lanterns and sparklers are not permitted.
  • Fixing things to surfaces. Bangarra's terms say nothing can be taped, tacked or rigged to walls or floors, so decor has to be free-standing. Parramatta Park's terms bar thumbtacks, sticky tape and nails on trees or facilities and require decorations to be removed after the booking.
  • Confetti, petals and balloons. Prohibited at several of these venues.
  • Set-up and pack-down time. At Sydney Olympic Park's Arc of Pines, hire time includes set-up and pack-down. At Bangarra, bump-in and bump-out are arranged in advance, may cost extra, and are subject to availability.

Sources: Bangarra Dance Theatre, weddings terms (2026); Sydney Olympic Park, Arc of Pines terms; Greater Sydney Parklands, wedding ceremony terms. These are examples, not your venue's rules.

If your venue finishes early, bump-out time matters. Our guide to curfews and package hours shows how much time packages really give you.

Run a one-table prototype

  1. Make one finished table: centrepiece, number, menu, place cards.
  2. Time it from start to finish and write down what it cost.
  3. Multiply by the number of tables. (As an illustration only: a 25-minute table across 12 tables is five hours.)
  4. Compare with a hire or buy quote. If the savings per hour of your time are small, buy it.

If you do go ahead, forum advice from couples is consistent on one thing: decorate the day before if you can access the venue, because you won't have time on the morning.

Your turn: the DIY shortlist

  • Each of you, write the three things you most want to make yourselves.
  • Run each through the four questions. Keep only those that score three or four.
  • Ask your venue for its decor rules and bump-in time. Write them next to each project.

Frequently asked questions

What wedding decor is worth DIYing?

Small, flat, make-ahead items such as programs, menus, place cards, table numbers and signs. Hire or buy large, tall or on-the-day items such as arches, backdrops and lighting.

How much does wedding decor cost in Australia?

Easy Weddings' 2026 report puts average decoration spend at $2,849 among couples who bought it. Bridebook says couples typically spend between $1,000 and $2,500 on professionally sourced decor, excluding flowers. The surveys differ, so treat it as a range.

Can I use candles at my wedding venue?

It depends on the venue. Bangarra Dance Theatre's 2026 wedding terms ban open flame including candles, and Sydney Olympic Park's wedding FAQ says candles, lanterns and sparklers are not permitted. Ask your venue for its terms.

Is DIY wedding decor really cheaper?

Sometimes. For some projects the materials cost as much as the finished item, and your time has a cost. Make one finished table, time it and cost it before you commit.
