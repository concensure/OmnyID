Drinks at your wedding: package, tab, BYO or cash bar?

Topics in this guide

couplesvenuescaterersguestsStage: Celebrant & suppliersAustraliacost anxietyclarity

Drinks are one of the largest line items at many weddings and one of the least predictable. In Australia the common set-ups are a beverage package, a bar tab and a BYO arrangement, with the cash bar less usual and more likely to raise eyebrows. Here is how each works and how to choose.

The main options

OptionHow it worksWatch for
Beverage packageFixed price per guest for a set number of hoursWhich drinks are included, what the cut-off time is, whether it applies to children and non-drinkers
Bar tab on consumptionYou pay for what is actually poured, often with a capEnding balance can be higher than expected; ask for a running total
BYOYou supply drinks, with or without corkageCorkage fees, glassware, staff and whether the venue allows it at all
Cash barGuests pay for their own drinksGuest reactions, card machines, queue times
HybridHosted drinks for a set time, then cash or limited optionsNeeds a clear handover moment so guests are not surprised

What decides the price

  • Hours. Every extra hour of a package costs more than it seems.
  • Drink tiers. Beer, wine and soft drinks cost less than spirits and cocktails. A signature cocktail plus beer and wine often feels generous without the full spirits bill.
  • Staffing. Bar staff must be trained in Responsible Service of Alcohol, and venues are required to follow liquor licensing rules, which affects what can be served.
  • Who holds the licence. If you BYO, ask who is responsible and what the venue requires.

How to keep the bill under control

  • Start the open bar after the ceremony and canapés, not the moment guests arrive.
  • Close the bar for the speeches and dinner service, then reopen.
  • Stop shots and top-shelf spirits if they push the budget.
  • Set a cap on a tab, with an agreed point when the venue checks in with you.
  • Offer good non-alcoholic options too. Many guests will notice.

If you choose a cash bar

A cash bar can be a reasonable choice when the alternative is cutting your guest list or running out of budget. The risk is that some guests expect hosted drinks, especially in Australia. To protect goodwill:

  • Say so clearly on your wedding website and invitations, not only at the door.
  • Provide something free: water, tea and coffee, a welcome drink and wine with dinner.
  • Make sure there is a card machine that works, with a signal, and some options at lower prices.
  • Make an effort to explain it to older guests in person.
  • Never put the cost on guests in a way that feels like a fundraiser.

For help with wording, see wedding websites and invitation wording.

Safety and hosting

You and the venue share an interest in guests getting home safely. Plan transport, such as a shuttle, taxi bookings or accommodation close by, and brief the MC to remind guests. A good venue will stop serving anyone who is intoxicated, and you should not ask them to ignore that rule.
