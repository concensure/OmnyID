Buying a wedding dress online in Australia, plus alterations and wearing what you like

Topics in this guide

couplesdressmakersalterations specialistsStage: Details & invitesAustraliauncertaintyexcitement

Online dress shopping can save money and widen your choices, but it also moves the risk onto you. A dress that does not fit in six months' time is a more serious problem than a shirt that is the wrong size. This guide covers how to shop carefully, what alterations really involve and how to choose something that is not a traditional dress.

Before you order online

  • Try on silhouettes in a local boutique first. Many boutiques charge an appointment fee, which is cheaper than buying the wrong shape. Take notes on which cut feels like you.
  • Measure yourself following the seller's guide, and ask whether they take custom measurements. Made-to-measure often arrives closer to your size than off-the-rack.
  • Order swatches of fabric and colour. Ivory, champagne and white look different on screen and in daylight.
  • Check reviews with real photos, not only the seller's own. Be cautious of heavily discounted designer copies; the shop may not be the designer's authorised retailer.

Terms to read first

  • Return and refund policy. Many made-to-order dresses are final sale.
  • Total cost in Australian dollars, including shipping, duties or GST on imported goods, and currency conversion fees.
  • Production and delivery times, and whether there is a rush fee.
  • Who to contact if the dress arrives damaged or differs from the photos. Overseas sellers can be harder to hold to account, so keep screenshots of the listing and your payment record.

Your rights under Australian Consumer Law can apply to goods bought online, but enforcing them against a seller overseas is harder in practice. Paying by card rather than bank transfer gives you a way to dispute a charge if the dress never arrives, though it is not a guarantee.

Timeline that works

WhenWhat to do
9 to 12 months beforeTry styles, set a budget, find an alterations seamstress
7 to 9 months beforeOrder, allowing for made-to-order production
3 months beforeDress arrives. Check it immediately and keep the packaging
6 to 8 weeks beforeFirst fitting with shoes and underwear you will actually wear
2 to 3 weeks beforeFinal fitting, steaming, bustle practice

These are common ranges, not rules. Ask both the seller and your seamstress for their own lead times.

Alterations: what to expect

  • Book your seamstress before the dress arrives, since good ones fill up in wedding season.
  • Expect more than one fitting. Hems, bust and waist adjustments and a bustle are common.
  • Ask for a written quote for the work, and a date for the final fitting.
  • Bring the shoes and any undergarments you will wear on the day.
  • Weight changes happen. Tell your seamstress and leave room in the schedule.

You do not have to wear a white dress

Non-traditional attire is increasingly common, and not only for brides. Options couples choose include a tailored suit or tuxedo, a jumpsuit, a two-piece set, a short dress for the reception, a coloured gown, or a family outfit with cultural meaning. A few points help it feel confident rather than compromised:

  • Choose what you would still like in photographs in 20 years, not what is popular this season.
  • Tell your photographer and your wedding party so colours do not clash.
  • Think about heat. Many Australian weddings are outdoors in summer, so fabric and layers matter.
  • Consider a second outfit for the reception, which can also be more comfortable for dancing.
