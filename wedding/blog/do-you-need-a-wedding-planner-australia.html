Do you need a wedding planner? How to decide, and what to ask in the first meeting

Topics in this guide

coupleswedding plannersStage: Plan & budgetAustraliaoverwhelmclarity

You do not need a wedding planner to have a good wedding, and plenty of couples plan every detail themselves. But a planner can save time, money and arguments in the right situation. The decision is easier when you know what each kind of planner does.

Three kinds of help

TypeWhat you getBest when
Full planningVenue and vendor sourcing, budget, timeline, design, on-the-day managementYou are time-poor, planning from overseas or hosting a large or complex wedding
Partial planningA set number of hours or tasks, such as vendor introductions and timeline reviewYou have done some of it and want expert checks
Day-of coordinationTakeover of the schedule, vendors and guests from a few weeks outYou are planning yourselves but do not want to run the day

Fees vary widely by state, experience and scope, so ask for a written quote rather than relying on an average. Some venues include a coordinator, but their job is the venue's side of the day, not yours. Ask what the venue coordinator does and does not manage.

Signs you probably do not need one

  • A small guest list at a venue with a full-service package.
  • You enjoy logistics and have a calm friend who can field questions on the day.
  • Your budget is tight enough that the fee would cost you a better photographer or venue.

Signs it will pay for itself

  • You are juggling multiple locations, such as a ceremony, reception and accommodation.
  • Family dynamics make decisions hard and you want a neutral person.
  • You are overseas or interstate and cannot visit vendors in person.
  • You keep losing track of who you have emailed. Vendor chasing can take over your evenings.

Questions for the first meeting

  1. What is included, and what costs extra? How are changes in scope handled?
  2. How many weddings do you take on in a season, and who covers if you are ill?
  3. Will you personally attend the wedding, or will an assistant?
  4. Do you have experience at our venue or with our type of wedding?
  5. Do you receive commissions or referral fees from vendors? If so, how is that disclosed?
  6. How do you work within a budget, and how do you report spending?
  7. How often will we meet, and what is your response time between meetings?
  8. What is your cancellation, postponement and refund policy, and do you carry public liability and professional indemnity insurance?
  9. Can we speak to two recent couples?
  10. What do you need from us, and what decisions do you expect us to make quickly?

Choosing between two

Skills matter, but fit matters more. You will be sharing stressful moments with this person. Pick the one who answers plainly, admits what they do not know and does not push a package before asking about your priorities. Compare the written proposals against the vendor fit checklist and read the contract carefully before paying a deposit.
