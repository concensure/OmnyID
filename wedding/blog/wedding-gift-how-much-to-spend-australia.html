How much should you spend on a wedding gift in Australia? What guests actually give

Topics in this guide

couplesguestsStage: Details & invitesAustraliauncertaintygenerosity

You've RSVP'd yes. Now comes the quiet question nobody likes asking out loud: how much do we give? There's no official rule, but there are survey figures and a few widely shared rules of thumb. This guide sets out what the data says, how couples can ask for what they want, and where the conversation gets tricky.

Key figures (2025 Australian Wedding Industry Report)

  • Close friends and relatives spent an average of $288 on a wedding present. Other guests spent $159.
  • Close friends and family were spending around $50 more than the year before.
  • 90% of couples had a wishing well for cash gifts at the reception.
  • A 2019 report from the same industry body, cited by Yahoo, gave $120 per couple for guests and $220 for close family or friends.

What guests spend

The most recent figures we found come from the 2025 Australian Wedding Industry Report, as reported in Australian regional newspapers (first published 25 July 2025, updated 8 September 2025). They are survey averages, not targets. Your relationship with the couple, your budget and the size of the wedding all matter more than an average.

The "cover your seat" rule of thumb

The same newspaper article quotes a frequent guest: "I think the unspoken rule for cash is usually to pay for your seat, meal and drinks." That's one person's view, not a survey result, and it won't suit every budget. It is a handy way to think about it, though. Check what a per-head price at the venue might be using our per-head data.

Destination and small weddings

The same article quotes a wedding planner saying the expectation of a large gift at a destination wedding is "significantly lower", because guests have already paid to travel, and that many couples say "your presence is our gift". If you've spent a lot to be there, it's reasonable to give less. A small, intimate wedding is different: the couple has invited you personally.

Cash, registry or something else?

  • Cash. The article reports that more couples are asking for cash instead of gifts. It also says a planner described cash as "always preferred" at a destination wedding.
  • Registry or honeymoon fund. Follow the couple's lead. A specific item you're told they want is always safe.
  • A shared gift. A group of friends can pool for something bigger.
  • Something personal. A framed photo or handwritten note costs little and often means a lot.

If you're the couple

The same article quotes a planner warning that relying on gifts of money to cover costs is "risky and could lead to financial stress if expectations aren't met". So treat gifts as a bonus, not a budget line. If you'd like cash, a short line on your wedding website helps, for example: "Your presence is the greatest gift. If you'd like to give something, we'd love a contribution to our honeymoon or home." See our budget guide for why leaving a buffer matters.

Do you give if you can't attend?

There is no rule. A card and a gift of any size is a kind gesture, and plenty of people give less than they would have if attending. Don't feel you have to match the average.

Your turn: the gift quick-check

  • Set a number you can give without stress.
  • Compare it with the averages above, and with the cost to attend.
  • Adjust for your relationship to the couple, then stop. Don't agonise.
