A 2027 wedding budget that includes the costs nobody lists

Topics in this guide

couplesvendorsStage: Plan & budgetAustraliacost anxietyclarity

Our post on budgeting without losing the joy covers the mindset. This guide is the working version: a structure you can copy, a list of costs that tend to be missed and a short answer on tipping.

Check your wedding budget

Step 1: set the total first

Decide what you can spend without borrowing for the day, including contributions from family. Then set a guest range, since guest count drives food, drinks, venue and hire costs. Choose your top three priorities and give them the largest share.

Step 2: a sample structure

CategoryPlanning bandBalanced starting target
Venue and ceremony26 to 30%28%
Catering and drinks19 to 25%22%
Photography and video10 to 14%12%
Attire, hair and makeup8 to 12%10%
Flowers, styling and hire6 to 10%8%
Music and entertainment4 to 8%6%
Celebrant, stationery, transport, rings and buffer11 to 17%14%

The starting targets add to 100%. The planning bands are alternatives for adjusting one category at a time, not percentages to add together. These are planning conventions, not market benchmarks, and your priorities should move them. If a venue package includes food or drinks, put each cost in only one category.

Costs couples forget

  • GST, service charges and card surcharges added to quotes that were shown without them.
  • Sunday and public holiday surcharges, and overtime if the night runs late.
  • Vendor meals for the photographer, videographer, DJ and planner.
  • Hire extras: chairs, linen, glassware, marquee, flooring, heating, fans and generators.
  • Alterations and a second fitting for attire.
  • Marriage paperwork and celebrant fees, which are covered in our legal checklist.
  • Accommodation and transport for you, your wedding party and family.
  • Thank-you gifts and favours (see favours guests actually keep).
  • Wedding insurance, which is worth pricing early.
  • Price rises between booking in 2026 and paying in 2027. Ask whether quotes are fixed.

Do you tip wedding vendors in Australia?

No, tipping is not expected. Most Australian vendors set their prices to include fair pay, and hospitality staff are covered by award wages. If someone has gone well beyond their role, there are better ways to say thanks than cash:

  • A written review on Google or the vendor's own site, which helps their business more than a small tip.
  • A meal and drinks for vendors who work through the night.
  • A handwritten thank-you note, and a gift for a small team such as the venue staff.
  • A tip or gift if you really want to, with no pressure. Some couples do, and it is welcome but not required.

Customs vary by culture and family, so ask your celebrant or planner if you are unsure. Some contracts include a service charge; check what it covers.

Step 3: track and recap

  • Keep columns for estimate, quote, deposit paid, final payment, due date and actual cost.
  • Update the sheet the same day you pay.
  • Review the budget every month and move money between categories on purpose, not by accident.
  • After the wedding, add a short recap of what you spent and what you would change. It helps friends planning their own day, and you will be glad you wrote it down.
