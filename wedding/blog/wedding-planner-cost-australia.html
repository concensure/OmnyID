Do you need a wedding planner? What day-of and full planning cost in Australia

Topics in this guide

coupleswedding plannersStage: Plan & budgetAustraliaoverwhelmclarity

"Do we need a wedding planner?" is one of the most common questions couples ask, and the honest answer depends on your time, your budget and how much you enjoy logistics. This guide sets out what planners charge in Australia, what each level of service includes, and a way to decide.

A disclosure. OmnyID builds planning software, so we benefit when couples plan themselves. We've tried to be fair, and every figure below is sourced.

Key figures

  • Day-of coordinator: $1,500 to $2,000 (Easy Weddings, quoting a planner).
  • Full-service planning: $5,000 to $6,000 (same source).
  • Hourly consultations: $75 to $100 per hour (same source).
  • Bridebook gives a wider $1,500 to $4,500, with fees often 10 to 15% of the wedding budget.

Easy Weddings also notes that a $6,000 full-service fee is "almost 17% of the average Australian wedding budget of $35315" (from the 2025 Wedding Industry Report). That's a big share of a budget, so be clear on what you'd be getting.

What each level includes

According to Easy Weddings:

  • Day-of coordinator: timeline management, vendor communication, set-up and pack-down, on-the-day problem-solving and guest coordination.
  • Full-service planner: end-to-end planning, supplier sourcing, design and styling, budgeting and payment schedules, and logistics and guest management, which may include transport, accommodation and favours.
  • Hourly consultations: advice on specific details.

Bridebook adds that partial planning, where a planner helps with specific tasks, is generally more affordable than full service.

How to decide: four questions

  1. How many hours can we realistically give? Planning a wedding is a lot of small tasks, and vendor emails and follow-ups take real hours. See our piece on vendor chasing fatigue.
  2. What would we pay to avoid it? If a coordinator's $1,500 buys you a calm day, that may be worth more than another hour of photography.
  3. Where is the risk? A complex venue, many suppliers, a destination or multiple cultural traditions make a planner more valuable.
  4. Does the venue already provide coordination? Some venues include a coordinator. Ask before you hire another.

The middle options

  • A day-of coordinator only. Easy Weddings suggests it to free up budget compared with full-service planning.
  • A few hours of consulting. Pay for advice on the parts you're stuck on.
  • A trusted friend or family member as day-of lead, with a written timeline and phone numbers.
  • A planning tool that keeps quotes, tasks and deadlines in one place.

Questions to ask a planner

  1. What is included, and what is billed separately?
  2. Do you take commissions or referral fees from vendors, and how do you disclose them?
  3. Who is my day-of contact, and are they the person I'm meeting?
  4. What does your contract say about cancellations? See your rights on deposits.
  5. Can I speak to a couple you've worked with?

Our guide to choosing the vendor that fits applies to planners too.

Your turn: the planner decision

  • Estimate the hours per week you can give. Multiply by the weeks to go.
  • Price what a coordinator and a full planner would cost in your city.
  • Decide which tasks you hate most. Outsource those first.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a wedding planner cost in Australia?

Easy Weddings, quoting a planner, gives about $1,500 to $2,000 for a day-of coordinator, $5,000 to $6,000 for full-service planning and $75 to $100 an hour for consultations. Bridebook gives $1,500 to $4,500 or 10 to 15% of the budget. Sources disagree.

What does a day-of wedding coordinator do?

Easy Weddings lists timeline management, vendor communication, set-up and pack-down, on-the-day problem-solving and guest coordination.

Is a wedding planner worth it?

It depends on your time, your budget and how complex the wedding is. A coordinator or a few hours of consulting can be a middle option.

What should you ask a wedding planner?

What's included, whether they take vendor commissions, who your day-of contact is, what the contract says about cancellation, and whether you can speak to past couples.
